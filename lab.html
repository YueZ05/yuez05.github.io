About the Lab

Our lab in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Southern University develops reliable, interpretable, and practical machine learning methods, with a focus on healthcare and biomedical data. We work closely with clinicians and domain scientists so that our models address real problems and can be used in practice.

Research Directions

1. Biomedical and Ophthalmic Image Analysis

We build deep learning methods for high-resolution medical imaging, such as adaptive optics OCT (AO-OCT) and ultrasound. Topics include cell and photoreceptor detection, retinal layer measurement, and automated montaging of ultrasound scans to recover ocular globe shape. We also use semi-supervised learning to reduce the amount of manual labeling these tasks need.

Related work: Semi-supervised RGC identification (BOE'24), ISOSNet (BOE'25), Ultrasound montaging

2. Clinical Waveform and Physiological Modeling

We model clinical time-series signals to estimate physiological properties and support diagnosis. For example, we use temporal mixture-of-experts models on Ocular Response Analyzer waveforms to predict corneal stiffness metrics and identify keratoconus.

Related work: Adaptive Temporal MoE (AJO'26)

3. Trustworthy and Fair AI for Healthcare

Medical AI must be accurate and also fair, calibrated, and understandable. We study bias in synthetic medical data and ways to mitigate it, and we use uncertainty quantification and interpretable modeling so clinicians can tell when to trust a prediction.

Related work: MedEqualizer

4. Graph Neural Networks and Representation Learning

We develop scalable representation learning methods for large and heterogeneous graphs, including multi-level frameworks that make network embedding efficient and accessible at scale.

Related work: HeteroMILE (WSDM'26 Companion), WebMILE (VLDB'22)

5. Interdisciplinary AI for Engineering and Science

Beyond healthcare, we apply data-driven modeling and intelligent systems to problems across engineering and the sciences, and we welcome collaborations that bring new data and new questions.
